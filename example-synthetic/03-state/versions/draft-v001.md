## Thesis

The deep ocean stores heat that the surface record cannot see, and the thermometers that watch it are rarely brought home. We argue that each deep float should report its own calibration and how sure we are of it, and that the means to do so are already in hand.
<!-- claims: FRAMING -->

## What traceable means

We call a deep heat-content estimate traceable when every float profile in it carries its own calibration adjustment with a stated uncertainty. A fleet-average adjustment alone does not qualify, and neither does an adjustment published without its uncertainty.
<!-- claims: DEF-01 -->

## The gap

Deep-float records below 2000 m are published without per-float calibration uncertainties. With only a fleet-average adjustment, the 2000-4000 m layer carries a residual warming trend error of 0.12 W m-2 [varga2024], and the cause is physical: creep of the pressure housing shifts the thermistor's reference resistance [okafor2025]. Laboratory recalibration cannot close this, because fewer than one in ten deep floats is ever recovered [ibarra2022].
<!-- claims: GAP-01, SUP-0001, SUP-0002, SUP-0003 -->

## The remedy and its cost

Calibrate every deep profile against a shared reference cast and publish the per-float adjustment with its uncertainty.
<!-- claims: REM-01-1, SUP-0004 -->

The cost is ship time, and the evidence covers one housing design: the result holds for the DeepProfiler-2 and Abyss-6K floats, which share it. Reference casts are scheduled opportunistically [quill2021], so the ask is scoped to floats with the tested housing.
<!-- claims: TEN-01, SUP-0005 -->

## The ask

We ask the deep-float data assembly centres to publish a per-float calibration adjustment and its uncertainty with every deep profile, derived from shared reference casts.
